HOW IT READS

It reads in chunks, not letters

Before anything else, your message is chopped into tokens — chunks of text, each swapped for a number.

  • IdeaCommon words are one chunk. Rare words break into pieces.
  • ThinkLike reading a sentence made of Lego bricks, not individual letters.
text → tokens

Antidisestablishment

03 / 08
WHERE CHUNKS COME FROM

Glue together what shows up most

Nobody picks the chunks by hand. A program reads a mountain of text and keeps gluing the most common pair of pieces into one.

  • StartEvery letter is its own piece.
  • Repeat"t" + "h" appear together a lot → glue into "th". Then "th" + "e" → "the".
  • StopAt ~100,000 chunks — the model's whole vocabulary. Fewer pieces than English has words, yet they snap together to spell any word, in any language.

04 / 08
WHY IT MATTERS

It never sees the letters

To the model, "strawberry" is just a few chunks. The r's are hidden inside them — which is why chatbots famously miscounted them.

  • FixNewer models often spell the word out first — s-t-r-a-w… — so each letter becomes its own token they can count.
  • AlsoChatbot limits and prices are counted in tokens — and code, emoji and other languages use more of them.

05 / 08
HOW IT WRITES

One chunk at a time

It's autocomplete, like on your phone — just much, much better. Guess the next chunk, add it, repeat.

  • IdeaFor every possible next chunk it gives a chance, then picks one.
  • NoteNo plan for the whole answer — every essay is built chunk by chunk.
"The cat sat on the ___"
mat61%
sofa18%
floor12%
moon2%

THE CREATIVITY DIAL

Temperature

A dial that decides how adventurous the pick is.

  • LowAlways the safest chunk — same answer every time.
  • HighUnlikely chunks sneak in — creative, and sometimes confidently wrong. That's a "hallucination".
same start, 3 tries each
